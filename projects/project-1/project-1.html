Project 1: Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection


Introduction

This project was inspired by the Russian photographer Sergei Mikhailovich Prokudin-Gorsky 1863-1944). Beginning in 1907, he traveled across the Russian Empire to take color photographs of everything he saw.

Naïve Approach

My approach for aligning the R, G, and B panels was to fix one of the panels (say, the blue panel) and align the other panels on top of it. This naive approach produced the following on one of the example images:
cathedral.jpg
We can see colors now in the image, but it's easy to tell that the red, blue, and green panels are not aligned. Note that I cropped all the images before stacking them.

Better Approach

To align the R, G, and B panels, I fixed one of the panels (the blue one) and shifted the other panels around to align them on top of it. When shifting the panels, I used one of two methods over a 30 x 30 pixel window to determine how "similar" two panels are on top of each other after shifting:
  • Euclidean distance
  • Normalized Cross Correlation - NCC

Even Better: Pyramid Method

While the previous method worked for the smaller jpg images, this method didn't work for the larger images due to the window for comparison being too small. We could increase the size of the window, but that would become expensive. Instead, we can use the image pyramid method.
